## Pre-conversation decisions

Three decisions framed pre-conversation per the design 7-Step arc precedent (Steps 3, 4, 5, 6 carried pre-conversation decisions).

**Decision 1: Audience scope.** Three audiences carry from the Step 6 Pass 3 close: case-study reader per bet line 67; senior leader deciding adoption per user-segment commitment; engineering team executing per internal-build audience. The decision: single audience, two audiences, or all three?

My read: case-study reader as the primary audience. The bet's strategic intent is the public demonstration of senior-product-leader-directs-enterprise-grade-build-through-Claude-Code; the case-study reader is the audience for whom this demonstration is constructed. The methodology-as-product proprietary insight at `charter/bet.md` is what the case study primarily demonstrates; Step 7 shapes the storyline to serve this audience. The senior-leader-deciding-adoption audience is secondary and partially served by the procurement-grade defensibility positioning already inherent in the storyline. The engineering-team-executing audience is already served by the charter content (packages.md, architecture.md, decisions.md, principles.md); duplicating that material at Step 7 adds noise rather than value.

Recommendation: case-study reader as primary; senior-leader-deciding-adoption as secondary if the conversation has bandwidth at Step 7 altitude; engineering-team audience explicitly out of scope (already served).

**Decision 2: Narrative density.** Executive summary versus full storyline versus mixed format. The Communicator's McKinsey override commits to "structured prose with executive summary" as default. The Pass 3 close named three surfaces (top-line plus arguments serves executive summary; arguments plus evidence trail serves full storyline; storyline tension surfaced and resolved serves substrate-coherence record).

My read: mixed format — executive summary opening plus structured prose body plus appendix-shaped evidence trail. The mixed format serves the case-study reader who wants to grasp the demonstration quickly (executive summary) plus drill into the demonstration's substance (body) plus verify specific claims (evidence trail). Pure narrative would lose the case-study reader who reads to evaluate, not to be persuaded; pure executive summary would miss the depth that procurement-grade defensibility requires; the mixed format respects both reading modes.

**Decision 3: Supporting artefact set commitments.** Three artefact categories carry from the Step 6 Pass 3 close.

Charter-pointer index: navigation aid for case-study readers wanting to drill into specific charter content. My read: ship at Step 7. Implementation cost is low (an index section); reader value is high.

Diagram set: the eight-package timeline showing Phase 2-A four waves into P13-P16 and Phase 2-B four waves into P17-P20 with dependency arrows; the eleven Phase 2-A sub-problem dependency graph; the six-architectural-primitives map. My read: package timeline as the must-ship diagram (single most useful artefact for the case-study reader; visualises the strategic shape directly); sub-problem dependency graph and primitives map deferred to Phase 2 build sessions when concrete reference points exist. Diagrams at Step 7 author at canonical altitude (mermaid in markdown; the charter's existing diagram convention); rendering for publication defers to wherever the case study lands.

Methodology-as-product pitch shape: the procurement-grade methodology-embedding positioning. My read: integrate into the executive summary opening as the case study's headline insight; do not author as a separate pitch deck. The methodology-embedding-as-proprietary-insight is the bet's core claim; the case study communicates it through the strategic-shape demonstration rather than through a standalone pitch.
