## DOCUMENT 3: LEADERSHIP MEMOS

*Four memos circulated to the leadership team in response to the CEO's pre-read. Reproduced with light editing.*

### Memo A: VP Product, Jordan Asare

The single most leveraged bet for Forgepath over the next 36 months is AI-powered matching and project intelligence. Our buyers currently spend an average of 11 days between posting a brief and engaging a supplier. Suppliers report that 42% of inbound briefs are underspecified or off-target. We are running a marketplace where both sides experience the matching layer as friction rather than value.

I am proposing a major investment in three product capabilities: (1) brief enrichment using LLMs and historical match data, (2) supplier ranking and recommendation, and (3) post-match project tracking with milestone and document workflows.

This is a defensible technical moat. It increases retention. It increases GMV per buyer. It compounds over time as our match data set grows.

### Memo B: VP Sales, Nestor Tellez

We are leaving money on the table by remaining vertical-narrow. Two of our top fifteen accounts have asked us, in the last quarter alone, whether we can source for their aerospace and defense divisions. The contract values in those segments are 4 to 6 times our current average. We have the operational capability. We do not have the credentials or the supplier base.

I am proposing a structured expansion into aerospace and defense services over the next 24 months, beginning with AS9100-certified supplier recruitment and ITAR-compliant project handling. This is not a small bet. It would require dedicated supply-side recruiting, compliance infrastructure, and a specialized sales pod. The payoff is access to deal sizes and account economics that fundamentally change the company's growth profile.

### Memo C: VP Operations, Lin Park

I want to make the unglamorous case. Our repeat buyer rate is 38%. Our NPS among buyers who do not repeat is 22. The single most common qualitative reason buyers do not return is supplier quality variability. We have a vetting process. It is not rigorous enough for the standards our enterprise-leaning buyers expect.

I am proposing a structured investment in supplier quality infrastructure: a tiered certification model, mandatory work-product sampling, performance scoring tied to buyer feedback and outcome data, and a category captain model in our top three service lines where a senior operations partner owns the supplier portfolio quality and depth.

This is not a sexy bet. It is the bet that turns transactional buyers into platform buyers. Every marketplace that has compounded at scale has solved this. We have not.

### Memo D: VP Growth, Priya Caldwell

We are underinvested in demand generation. Brand awareness in our target buyer segment is 8% unaided. We are losing efficient acquisition opportunities to the noise of category education. Buyers who do find us convert well, but the top of the funnel is too narrow and too dependent on outbound and referral.

I am proposing a 24-month investment in category leadership: industry analyst engagement, a flagship annual conference, a content engine targeting plant managers and engineering directors, and a coordinated brand campaign in the three geographic regions with the highest concentration of mid-market manufacturers.

I would also ask the team to seriously consider expanding into two adjacent service categories where we have leading-indicator demand: regulatory and compliance consulting, and supply chain network design. Both are areas where current buyers ask us if we can help.
