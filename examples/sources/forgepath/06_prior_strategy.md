## DOCUMENT 2: INVESTOR LETTER FROM SERIES C LEAD

**From:** Daniel Verhoeven, Partner, Threshold Capital
**To:** Anika Rao
**Date:** November 11
**Subject:** Following up on our last conversation

Anika,

Thanks for the time on Tuesday. I want to put a few thoughts in writing ahead of your offsite, both because I think they will be useful and because I want to be transparent about what I will be looking for at the January board meeting.

The Series C thesis we underwrote was specific. We backed Forgepath because the data showed that mid-market manufacturers in North America have a structurally underserved sourcing problem in specialized services, and because Forgepath had achieved real liquidity in CAD/CAM, industrial design, and quality testing. We did not back a generalist services marketplace. We did not back a fintech. We did not back European expansion.

I am sharing this because the vision draft you sent reads, to me, like a fundraising deck rather than a strategic plan. "Operating system for industrial services" is the kind of phrase that sells a round. It is not the kind of phrase that helps a leadership team decide what not to do.

A few specific challenges I would put to the team at the offsite:

First, repeat buyer rate is 38%. That is the number I am watching most closely. Marketplaces that do not solve repeat usage do not compound. Every conversation I have heard from your customer team points to post-match friction. The match is good. What happens after the match is where the platform falls short. That sounds to me like the most important thing you are not yet doing.

Second, supplier acquisition is currently linear and people-intensive. You have a 2,400-supplier base built largely through outbound sales and event-driven recruiting. To get to the scale your model implies, supplier acquisition needs to be a system, not a sales motion. I have not seen a credible plan for that.

Third, on the fintech adjacency. I am skeptical. Embedded financing in B2B marketplaces is a graveyard for product teams unless the marketplace has dominant share. You do not have dominant share. I would not bet capability investment on it at this stage. Partner if you must.

I will support whatever direction the team commits to, provided the commitment is real and the trade-offs are explicit. What I will not support is a plan that promises everything.

Daniel
